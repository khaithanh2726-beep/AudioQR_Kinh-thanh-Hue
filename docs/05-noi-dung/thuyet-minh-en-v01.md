## 1. Ngo Mon Gate

This is the main gate on the southern side of Hue Imperial City, built by Emperor Minh Mang in 1833. The gate has five passageways, with the central entrance reserved exclusively for the emperor. The Five Phoenix Pavilion above the gate was once a venue for major court ceremonies, including military reviews and ceremonies announcing the successful candidates of imperial examinations. It also witnessed Emperor Bao Dai’s abdication in 1945, marking the end of Vietnam’s monarchy.

<a id="thai-hoa"></a>

## 2. Thai Hoa Palace

Thai Hoa Palace symbolizes the supreme authority of the Nguyen dynasty and houses the imperial throne. It hosted the country’s most important ceremonies, including coronations, imperial birthday celebrations and regular court audiences. Its architecture is distinguished by a distinctive arrangement of overlapping roofs and connected halls, known as “trung thiem diep oc.” Elaborately carved dragon motifs throughout the palace represent the solemn authority of the throne.

<a id="tu-cam-thanh"></a>

## 3. Forbidden Purple City

Located at the centre of the complex, directly behind Thai Hoa Palace, the Forbidden Purple City was the secluded private living area of the emperor and the royal family. Although many buildings suffered severe damage during historical upheavals, the surviving foundations and ongoing restoration projects still help visitors imagine the luxurious, enclosed setting and the strict rules that once governed life within the royal household.

<a id="cuu-dinh"></a>

## 4. Nine Dynastic Urns

The Nine Dynastic Urns are nine monumental bronze vessels cast during Emperor Minh Mang’s reign and placed in front of The Mieu Temple. Bearing names associated with the emperors’ posthumous titles, the urns feature 162 finely cast relief motifs depicting mountains, rivers, local products and cultural treasures from across Vietnam’s three regions. They are regarded as an encyclopedia cast in bronze, affirming the country’s unity and enduring authority.

<a id="dien-tho"></a>

## 5. Dien Tho Residence

Dien Tho Residence is an extensive architectural complex built as a home for the empress dowagers of the Nguyen dynasty. It preserves the artistic qualities of traditional royal architecture and is among the rare palace complexes that suffered relatively little destruction during wartime. It also bears witness to the emperors’ respect for their mothers, reflecting the importance of filial piety in Confucian tradition.
